# «По счёту — для компаний» — what was built, what was decided, what is open

06.09.2026, branch `r-invoice` (five commits on top of `1a63cf8`). Scope: the
fourth checkout payment method, end to end — the company form, the numbered
invoice, its PDF and letter, the admin's «Отметить оплаченным», the accountant
export, the docs and the tests. Nothing outside the invoice path was touched;
`payNow()`, `POST /api/payments/create/` and `src/lib/payments/settle.ts` belong
to the payments agent and were deliberately left alone (see «Boundaries»).

Written for Dim. §4 is the list of decisions that need an answer — everything in
it is *implemented* with a sensible default, so nothing is blocked; the answers
change a setting or a few lines, not the shape.

Two design points worth keeping in mind when reading the code:

- **The invoice is not the payment record.** `orders.payment` is rewritten on
  the paid transition (`src/lib/payments/apply.ts`); the invoice number has to
  survive that, so it lives in its own column.
- **Marking paid is not a shortcut.** It goes through `applyPaymentResult()`,
  the same function a Montonio webhook goes through — so the gift card, the
  promo use, the loyalty points, the stock movement and the «Заказ принят»
  letter happen exactly once, and a second press is a no-op. That is what the
  «settles exactly once» test asserts, twice over (spy counts and audit rows).

**The 0 € guard** (the payments agent's boundary note). A basket a gift card or
the loyalty points already cover in full has nothing to invoice, and a счёт for
0 € is not a document anybody can pay or book. Two doors: the checkout drops
«По счёту» from the method list the moment `total()` reaches zero (and the pick
falls back to the bank link, so the radio on screen is the method that is sent),
and `createOrder()` refuses such an order with `invoice_zero_total` — a stale tab
cannot talk its way past it. Nothing about the payments agent's own zero-total
path was touched; when it lands, a 0 € order simply never enters this branch.

## 3. The legal answer, in plain terms

**Taking the order** needs almost nothing: a company name and a registry code
identify the buyer well enough to sell to them.

**Issuing a compliant Estonian invoice** needs more. The Value-Added Tax Act
§ 37 lists what an invoice must carry, and the buyer's **address** is on that
list — which is why the checkout asks for the legal address and does not just
take the delivery address silently. (It offers it: the box «Юридический адрес
совпадает с адресом доставки» is on by default when there is a courier address,
so a company that ships to its own office types nothing extra.)

The buyer's **VAT number (KMKR)** is *optional* for a domestic Estonian sale —
the invoice is valid without it — and *needed* for an intra-EU B2B sale, where
it is the thing that lets the seller apply the reverse charge (0 % Estonian VAT,
the buyer accounts for VAT at home). Hence: an optional field, validated for
shape when filled, never required.

What the invoice itself must carry, all of which the PDF prints:

- seller: name, address, registry code, VAT number;
- buyer: name and address (VAT number when given);
- a **unique sequential number** and the **issue date**;
- the **due date** (the payment term);
- line items with quantity, unit price, VAT rate and VAT amount;
- **net total, VAT total, grand total**, and the currency;
- how to pay: the **IBAN** (with the bank) and the **payment reference** —
  here «номер заказа, номер счёта», which is what the owner matches on the bank
  statement.

Two consequences that are already in the code: the number is allocated by the
database in a single statement (a sequential number that two invoices could
share would not be a sequential number), and prices in this shop include VAT, so
every figure on the invoice is **backed out** of the gross line
(net = gross / 1,24) instead of being added on top — which is also why the lines
foot to the exact sum the customer is asked to transfer.

Not implemented, deliberately: **e-arve** (the Estonian machine-readable
e-invoice). The business register says Rempire Store OÜ does not accept
e-invoices; a PDF by e-mail is what a salon expects. See question 8.

## 4. Decisions taken — please confirm

Each of these is implemented with the default in **bold**. Changing most of them
is a setting, not a rebuild.

1. **Numbering scheme — `A-2026-0001`: prefix + year + four digits, restarting
   at 1 every January.** The prefix («A-») and nothing else is editable in
   «Настройки → О компании → Счета для компаний». Alternative: one unbroken run
   that never restarts. Does Renat's accountant expect a particular shape?
2. **Payment term — 7 days**, editable 1–60 in the same card, quoted to the
   shopper in the checkout («оплата в течение 7 дней»). Salons normally get 7 or
   14; 14 is a one-field change.
3. **Nothing ships before the money arrives.** The order sits in «новый», the
   card says «отправлять пока нечего», and the «Заказ принят» letter goes out on
   the paid transition, not before. Alternative for trusted salons: allow the
   owner to create a label on an unpaid invoice order.
4. **Anyone may choose «По счёту»** — the method is in the list for every
   shopper, not gated on an approved partner/salon account. The exposure is
   small (nothing ships until the transfer lands), and the alternative — only
   approved partner accounts, which the wholesale/loyalty work already has —
   is a two-line condition once you say the word. My worry with gating it now:
   a new salon's very first order is exactly the one that wants an invoice.
5. **Invoice language — Estonian + English on every invoice, plus Russian on a
   Russian-language order.** Headings and the line words are bilingual («Tarne /
   Delivery»), so one document works for a local accountant and for a foreign
   one. Alternative: follow the order's language only.
6. **No auto-cancel.** An unpaid invoice stays open forever; the admin shows
   «Просрочен на N дней» and lists it in «Сделать сегодня», and the owner
   cancels by hand. Auto-cancel after N days (14? 30?) plus a reminder letter
   on day N is the obvious next step — say the word and the numbers.
7. **VAT is 24 % for every buyer, including a company in another EU country**
   with a valid VAT number. The reverse charge (0 % + «Pöördmaksustamine /
   Reverse charge, Art. 196 Directive 2006/112/EC» on the invoice) is *not*
   implemented. This is the one item on the list with a real tax consequence,
   and it needs Renat's accountant, not us: how often does he sell to
   VAT-registered companies outside Estonia?
8. **Estonian e-Business Register autocomplete — later, not now.** Typing a
   registry code and having the name, address and KMKR fill themselves in is a
   nice touch, and `ariregister.rik.ee` has an API, but it needs a contract and
   a key; there is a free JSON export of the whole register that could be
   shipped as a lookup file instead. Same bucket: **e-arve** (§ 3).
9. **The seller address on the invoice — «Mardi 1, 10145 Tallinn»**, taken from
   «Настройки → О компании», never hard-coded. The business register has
   Pärnu mk, Paikuse alev, Käärasoo tee 43, 86604. Renat already chose Mardi 1
   for documents in round 2 (`docs/RENAT-ANSWERS.md`, 23.08.2026), and an
   earlier line in that same file still calls the choice unresolved and says
   invoice templates are blocked on it. The code follows the setting either way
   — it needs one confirmation, not a code change.
10. **A 0 € order cannot be an invoice order** (§ 2). If a gift card covers
    everything, the method disappears from the checkout.

## 5. Risks and known gaps

| # | Risk | Why it matters | Cheapest fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **An invoice with no IBAN.** The IBAN and the bank name are blank in the settings today. | The company gets a valid-looking invoice with nothing to pay it into. The admin card warns («В блоке «Реквизиты» выше не заполнено: …») and the PDF says it out loud rather than printing a blank — but nothing *blocks* the first sale. | Fill them in. Or refuse the method until they are set — one condition, say the word. |
| 2 | **Nothing reserves stock while an invoice is unpaid.** The stock movement happens on the paid transition. | A company orders the last three bottles, pays a week later, and the shelf sold them meanwhile. | Accept (small shop, few invoice orders), or decrement on issue and return on cancel — a bigger change that touches the inventory agent's area. |
| 3 | **Number gaps are possible.** The serial is allocated before the record is written; a database failure in between burns a number. | Some accountants dislike a gap in a sequential run. It is rare and explainable (the audit trail has the allocation). | Leave it; note it if the accountant asks. |
| 4 | **An order can exist without its invoice.** If `nextInvoiceNumber()` fails, `createOrder()` throws *after* the row is written — the shopper sees an error and may order again, and the orphan row has a company but no invoice. There is **no admin button to issue an invoice for an order that has none.** | Rare (a database outage), but the recovery is manual SQL today. | A «Выписать счёт» action on the order card, reusing `issueInvoice()`. ~20 lines. |
| 5 | **No credit note, no correction.** Cancelling an order leaves its invoice standing, and an issued invoice cannot be amended. | If a company pays a wrong invoice, the fix is on paper. | A credit-note PDF is a whole feature; flag it and decide after the first month. |
| 6 | **«Отметить оплаченным» trusts the owner.** No bank-statement reconciliation. | A mis-click marks an unpaid order paid, which ships goods and spends a gift card. | It is behind a confirm card and is a no-op the second time; the audit trail records who and when. Fine at this scale. |
| 7 | **The PDF needs its fonts on the deployed image** (the gift card's — the same files). Without them the route answers `503 not_configured` and the letter goes out without the attachment rather than failing. | A deploy that loses the fonts loses the attachment silently for the customer. | The e2e asserts a real `%PDF-` body on every run; keep it in CI. |
| 8 | **Resend de-duplicates on the idempotency key for 24 h.** The first send is keyed on the invoice number, «ещё раз» on the moment it was pressed. | Two resends in the same second would collapse into one. | Intended; noted so it is not mistaken for a bug. |
| 9 | **Merge boundary.** `payNow()`, `POST /api/payments/create/` and `src/lib/payments/settle.ts` were not edited — but the branch does touch `public/shop2/app.js` (checkout + admin) and `src/lib/orders.ts`'s `createOrder()`, both of which the payments work also lives in. | A conflict at merge time is likely in `app.js`, and it will be textual and small. | Merge invoice first or last, not in parallel; the tests here are the safety net. |
| 10 | **`tests/invoices.test.ts` and `src/emails/invoice.ts` had raw NUL bytes** written into them as string sentinels, which made git treat both files as **binary** — no diff, no line-level merge. Fixed in `9adf6b1` (escapes instead of raw bytes; same values). | Worth knowing it happened: any file written that way silently loses mergeability. | Done. |

company name, places the order with no bank page in between, lands on «Заказ
оформлен» with «Счёт отправлен на …» and survives a reload; then the owner finds

downloads a response that really is a PDF (fetched with the page's own cookies),
sees the letter with the PDF's filename in the e2e mail sink, resends it, and
marks it paid — after which the order is `paid`, the customer's «Заказ принят»
went out **once**, and the invoice is still there to download. The storefront
half runs on mobile-safari too; the admin half stays Chromium-only, as
`docs/testing.md` requires.
