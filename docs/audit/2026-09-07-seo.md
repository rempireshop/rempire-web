## 2. The redirects — the single biggest risk, now covered

`docs/redirect-map.csv` was the stated source of truth and was implemented
nowhere. Reading it settled that it should not be implemented as written:

* it has **1 639 rows**, not the 1 638 `docs/seo.md` claims (the file ends
  without a newline, so `wc -l` undercounts it by one);
* **1 447 of them point at `/shop/`** — the home page — including *every*
  `/ru/products/…` and `/et/products/…` row, which is 627 impressions and 13 of
  the 47 clicks;
* only 186 rows resolve to a real product page;
* two of its five info targets (`right-to-return`, `data-sharing-opt-out`) are
  not slugs the shop has.

So the map is kept as a **test fixture**, not as run-time data. The rules are in
`src/lib/legacy-redirects.ts` and `tests/legacy-redirects.test.ts` walks all
1 639 rows through them and asserts every one lands on a page the shop serves.

### Where it runs, and why not in `next.config.ts`

`src/middleware.ts`, matched to the Shopify shapes only. Three reasons:

1. **It is a lookup, not a pattern.** `/products/<handle>` is answered by asking
   the catalogue whether that handle is a product. A config rule can only
   rewrite one shape into another, so covering the map there would mean 1 639
   literal rules.
2. Those rules would live in the routes manifest and be matched, in order, on
   **every** request to the site — beside the ~250 rewrites
   `prerenderedRewrites()` already emits, and inside Vercel's route budget. The
   middleware loads only for the paths in its `matcher`, none of which the new
   shop uses.
3. The query string has to be dropped per shape, and `/search`'s `q` kept. That
   is code.

### What it covers

Measured against the 473 URLs Search Console has impressions for:

| rule | URLs | impressions | clicks |
|---|---|---|---|
| `product` — the handle is a catalogue id | **403** | **2 486** | 25 |
| `collection-brand` — the collection is a brand we carry | 14 | 251 | 2 |
| `collection-cat` — the collection is a section | 38 | 134 | 0 |
| `home` — a bare `/ru`, `/et`, `/en-fi` | 2 | 64 | 4 |
| `info` — `/pages/…`, `/policies/…` | 7 | 24 | 0 |
| `product-alias` — a product that is gone, its line is not | 4 | 19 | 1 |
| `blog` — the old blog | 2 | 7 | 0 |
| `screen`, `product-unknown` — the two fallbacks | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| `/` — owned by `next.config.ts`'s existing 307 | 1 | 152 | 15 |
| **total** | **473** | **3 139** | **47** |

**432 of the 473 URLs — 2 851 of the 3 139 impressions — land on the exact page
the searcher was looking for.** Nothing 404s.

The rules, in one paragraph: the language prefix is read first (`/ru/…` →
unprefixed, because Russian is the shop's default and `/shop2/ru/…` would only
be 301ed again; `/et/…` → `/shop2/et/…`; `/en-lv`, `/en-lt` and `/en-fi` all
collapse onto the one `/shop2/en/…` the shop has). Then the shape: `products` →
the product page when the handle is a catalogue id, else an alias, else the
brand page when the handle starts with a brand slug, else «Все товары»;
`collections` → the brand page, else one of the seven sections, else «Все
товары» (105 Shopify collection handles are mapped by hand, in four languages'
worth of spelling); `pages` and `policies` → the five info pages, an unknown one
to «Контакты»; `blogs` → the blog listing; `search` keeps `q`; `cart` and
`checkout` → «Все товары», because the basket is a drawer here and the checkout
is robots-disallowed. Everything is a **301**.

Two details worth naming:

* **The query string is dropped.** `variant` names a Shopify variant id that
  means nothing here, `country=AE&currency=EUR` would be read as a shop setting,
  and the `utm_` set describes a feed that stops existing at the switch —
  carrying it over would credit new traffic to a dead channel. This is what
  collapses the 61 duplicated paths onto one address each.
* **A percent-escaped handle is decoded and re-slugified**, so Shopify's
  `/products/captain-fawcett-barberism%C2%AE-beard-oil` finds
  `captain-fawcett-barberism-beard-oil` instead of 404ing on a `®`.

### What it cannot do

1. **Two hops, not one.** `trailingSlash: true` means Next answers
   `/products/night-rider` with a 308 to `/products/night-rider/` *before* any
   middleware runs, and the 301 follows. Every URL Google holds is the no-slash
   form, so every one of them is a 308 → 301 chain. This is not new — every
   `/shop/…` redirect in `next.config.ts` has always behaved this way — and
   Google follows and consolidates chains of this length without complaint. The
   one-line fix, if it ever matters, is `skipTrailingSlashRedirect: true` plus
   handling the normalisation ourselves; that would change every `/shop2/` path
   too, so it is not worth doing for this.
2. **A discontinued product is a soft 404 by design.** 219 old handles are not
   in the catalogue. They 301 to their brand's page or to «Все товары». Google
   reads a 301 onto a page that is not the same thing as "this is gone" and
   drops the address after a while — which is the correct outcome, and better
   than a 404 for the shopper who clicked. Only two of them still earn anything
   (`blow-dry-ever-thicken`, `blow-dry-ever-bounce`), and both are aliased to
   `blow-dry-ever-smooth`, the surviving product in the same line.
3. **The `en-lv`/`en-lt`/`en-fi` cluster loses its regional split.** Google
   knows three regional English storefronts; the shop has one English page. 79
   impressions and zero clicks across all three, so nothing is lost, but Google
   will spend a few weeks folding three URLs into one.
4. **`/` is not ours.** The site root is a 307 to `/shop2/` from
   `next.config.ts`, deliberately temporary because the shop is expected to
   *become* the root later. It is the best-earning URL on the property (15 of 47
   clicks) — see question 1.
5. **It cannot bring back a ranking that was earned by a page whose content
   changed.** A `/collections/system-4` that ranked on 40 products' worth of text
   now lands on a brand page with the same products but different copy. The
   ranking will move, up or down, and no redirect decides which way.

### hreflang

Google currently knows six addresses per product (unprefixed, `/ru`, `/et`,
`/en-lv`, `/en-lt`, `/en-fi`). The new pages declare four: `ru` (unprefixed),
`et`, `en`, and `x-default` on the unprefixed Russian one. The redirects encode
exactly that collapse, so the cluster Google re-learns is the cluster the pages
declare, and a searcher who picked Estonian stays in Estonian. Verified end to
end in `e2e/seo.spec.ts`.

## 3. Titles, descriptions and structured data

### The bug first

`stripTags()` in `src/lib/seo-head.mjs` stripped tags but did not decode
entities, and the meta description is escaped once on the way out. Gatsby's
«Mat &amp; Hard Lift-Up Wax» was published to Google as
«Mat &amp;amp; Hard Lift-Up Wax». `tools/build-merchant-feed.mjs` has decoded
entities since it was written; the SEO head never did.

| | before | after |

| description | `НЕВЕСОМЫЙ ЛАК ДЛЯ ВОЛОС — ОБЪЁМ И БЛЕСК БЕЗ МАСЕЛ ОТ KEVIN MURPHY Бросьте вызов гравитации — сделайте укладку ещё объёмнее и красивее с ANTI.GRAVITY.SPRAY…` | `Бросьте вызов гравитации — сделайте укладку ещё объёмнее и красивее с ANTI.GRAVITY.SPRAY. Наш лёгкий… · от 8 € · в наличии · доставка по Эстонии и Балтии` |

Three changes, all in `src/lib/seo-head.mjs` and mirrored word for word in
`public/shop2/app.js`'s `setHead()` — Googlebot reads the rendered DOM, so a
change in only one of them would be a change in nothing:

1. **The title's middle rung.** The ladder was: the full sentence
   («… — купить в Rempire · 15 €») if it fits in 60 characters, else
   «… — REMPIRE», else the bare name. 123 of the 220 products fell to
   «— REMPIRE», which spends ten characters on a word Google prints beside every
   result anyway (from `og:site_name` and the `WebSite` block). The new rung is
   brand + name + price. Counted over the whole catalogue in Russian:
   **44 titles carried a price before, 166 do now**; the «— REMPIRE» ending
   went from 123 titles to 1; the remaining 53 are products whose name alone
   leaves room for neither. No title is over 60 characters, before or after.
2. **The shouted heading goes.** A product text that opens with a run of
   capitals — «BIO BOTANICAL SERUM ОТ SYSTEM 4 Сыворотка…» — used to publish
   that run as the first 30 characters of the snippet. It is dropped, and the
   sentence after it starts the description. A text that never reaches a normal
   sentence is left alone.
3. **The snippet ends on the three facts a shopper is deciding on** — price,
   stock, delivery — and the tail is budgeted *first*, so it is never the half
   that gets cut off. A pair the owner or the assistant wrote by hand is left
   exactly as written; no price is appended to a sentence somebody chose.

Pinned by `tests/seo-snippet.test.ts` (14 tests), including a check that
`app.js` still carries the same regex, the same budget and the same ET/EN
wording as `seo-head.mjs`.

### Out of stock

«Gatsby Moving Rubber Grunge Mat» is out of stock (100 impressions in the week,
80 of them on the plain URL at position 10.96). We **do** already tell Google:
the offer says `https://schema.org/OutOfStock`, and it did before this branch.
What changed:

* the meta description now says «нет в наличии» in the searcher's own language,
  so nobody clicks through to a disappointment;
* its sitemap priority drops from 0.7 to 0.4.

It **keeps** its page, its index directive and its rich result. The page still
answers the question that was asked, it carries «Сообщить о наличии», and a
ranking thrown away has to be earned again when the stock comes back. Google
suppresses an `OutOfStock` product from the merchant surfaces by itself — that
is what the availability field is for.

### The brand query

`rempire` — 141 impressions, 11 clicks, position **3.79**. A shop ranks for its
own name on the strength of what the site says the name *is*. The
`Organization` block said five things and none of them was a name. It now
carries `alternateName` («Rempire», «Rempire Shop», «rempireshop», «Rempire
Store OÜ»), `legalName`, `email`, `telephone`, `vatID`, `taxID`, `areaServed`,
an `image`, and an `@id` — and it is typed `["Organization", "Store"]`, because
there is a counter at Mardi 1 and the checkout offers pickup from it. Every
value comes from `src/data/content.default.json`, the same place the footer and
the invoices read, so there is nothing here to keep in step by hand.

The `WebSite` block gained `alternateName`, an `@id` and a `publisher`
pointing at the Organization node, so the three language home pages describe one
entity instead of three. **No `SearchAction`**: Google retired the sitelinks
searchbox it fed, so it would be markup nobody reads.

## 4. What was deliberately *not* added

Two things would help the merchant listings more than anything above, and both
are blocked on a fact nobody here can invent:

* **`hasMerchantReturnPolicy`** would put "Free 30-day returns" under the
  result. The refund policy (`public/shop/legal.js`) promises a 30-day window
  and a return label — and then excludes "personal care goods (such as beauty
  products)", which is most of the catalogue. Publishing structured data the
  policy text contradicts is worse than publishing none. See question 5.
* **`shippingDetails`** would put "Free delivery" under it. The facts exist
  (`5.47 €` for an Estonian parcel, free from `59 €`) but they differ by carrier
  and by country, and a wrong shipping figure in structured data is the kind of
  mistake that costs a Merchant Center account. See question 6.

`priceValidUntil` was left off on purpose: it is a promise with a date on it,
and Google treats a past one as an expired offer.

## 5. The AI prompts

Dim: «Every text generated with AI / Assistant needs to be perfect for SEO.»
The prompts said *«at most 60 characters»* and *«no trailing "| Rempire"»* and
nothing else — no shape for the title, no account of what the shop appends by
itself, and no word about the fact that the front of a product description **is**
the meta description of 220 pages.

`src/lib/ai-prompts.ts` now carries two shared blocks. `SEO_RULES` reaches
`seo` (product and post), `blog_outline`, `post_full` and `post_translate`;
`SEO_PRODUCT_RULES` reaches only a product's snippet. A customer reply and a
product-text translation get neither — they are not search results.

**Before** (the whole of the product `seo` task):

> - "title": an SEO title, at most 60 characters INCLUDING spaces — count them. Include the product type and brand naturally, no keyword stuffing, no trailing "| Rempire" (the site appends that itself).
> - "description": an SEO meta description, at most 155 characters INCLUDING spaces — count them. A concrete, specific reason to click, not a repeat of the title.

**After** (the shared rules, then the task):

> HOW THIS SHOP'S SEARCH RESULTS WORK — the budget you are writing to:
> - A Google result prints about 60 characters of the title and about 155 of the description. Both are hard limits: count the characters INCLUDING spaces and stay inside them. What runs past is cut mid-word, and a result that ends mid-word reads as a broken page.
> - The shop adds " — REMPIRE" to a title of 50 characters or fewer by itself, and Google prints the site's name beside every result anyway. Never write "Rempire", "| Rempire", "REMPIRE" or the domain into a title yourself.
> - One title, one subject. A title naming two products, or a product and a category, ranks for neither.
> - No keyword stuffing: never repeat a word to fit it in twice, never chain synonyms ("шампунь, шампуни, средство для мытья волос"), never bolt on a city, a country or "купить" where the sentence does not need it.
> - The description is a reason to click, not a second title and not a slogan. Say what the thing is and who it is for, then the one concrete thing about it that would decide a purchase — taken from INPUT, never invented. No call to action ("Купите сейчас", "Заказывайте"), no exclamation marks, no ALL-CAPS.
> - Never write a price, a discount, a delivery time or a stock figure into either field. The shop puts the live price and "в наличии" into the result itself, from its own data; a number saved inside a text is wrong the first time it changes.
>
> WHAT PEOPLE ACTUALLY TYPE — write the title in their words, in their order: the brand, then the maker's own name for the line, then what the thing is. The shop's real queries look like this: "system 4 bio botanical shampoo", "kevin murphy anti.gravity spray", "davines naturaltech calming shampoo", "mandom gatsby moving rubber grunge mat hair wax 80g", "system 4 t scalp tonic". So keep the maker's spelling exactly as INPUT gives it — the dots in "ANTI.GRAVITY.SPRAY", the line name ("Naturaltech", "Moving Rubber", "Bio Botanical"), and the size when the maker's own name carries one ("80 g"). Do not translate, transliterate or re-case any of it.
>
> TASK: write the Google search snippet for this product, in Russian.
> - "title": at most 60 characters INCLUDING spaces — count them. Brand, then the maker's name for the product, then what it is, in Russian: "System 4 Bio Botanical Shampoo — шампунь", "Kevin.Murphy ANTI.GRAVITY.SPRAY — спрей для объёма".
> - "description": at most 155 characters INCLUDING spaces — count them. One sentence on what it is and whom it suits, one on the thing about it that decides the purchase. Nothing that is not in INPUT.

Every query in that list is a real one out of the export.

Two more prompts were told a fact about the shop they did not have:

* **`describe`** — "THE FIRST SENTENCE IS THE SEARCH SNIPPET. When nobody writes
  a separate Google description for this product, the shop cuts the page's meta
  description out of the front of this text. So sentence one has to stand on its
  own in a search result: under 100 characters, saying plainly what the product
  is and who it is for. Do not open with a heading, with the brand shouted back
  at the reader, or with a line in capitals."
* **`copy` / `product_name`** — "This name is not only a label: the shop builds
  the product page's `<title>` out of the brand and this name, so it is what the
  page will rank for. Keep every word a buyer would type — the maker's own line
  name, the maker's own punctuation, and the size when the maker's name carries
  one. Do not tidy, translate or shorten those, and do not add words of your own
  to help it rank."

`blog_outline`'s six H2s are now asked to read like questions a reader would
ask; `post_translate` is told that Estonian and English run longer than Russian
and to re-write a title to fit rather than let it overflow.

## 6. Для Рената — что будет после переезда

**Коротко: сейчас магазин находят, но почти всегда не те люди.**

За неделю (29 августа — 5 сентября) магазин показался в Google **3 002 раза** и
получил **47 переходов**. Это мало, но дело не в том, что сайт плохой.

* Из 3 002 показов только **335** пришлись на Эстонию, Латвию, Литву и
  Финляндию — то есть на те страны, куда мы возим. Оттуда пришло **19
  переходов из 47**: это 5,7 % — нормальный, здоровый показатель.
* Остальные 2 667 показов — это США, Нидерланды, Россия, Германия, ОАЭ,
  Филиппины. Люди видят товар, но заказать его не могут, поэтому и не нажимают.
* Отдельная история — **736 показов «на первом месте» без единого перехода**.
  Это не поиск. Это бесплатные карточки товаров, которые Shopify отправлял в
  Google Покупки, и **713 из них показывались покупателям в Объединённых
  Арабских Эмиратах**. Первое место в списке товаров для Дубая — красивая
  цифра, которая ничего не стоит.
* С телефона магазин показывается на шесть позиций выше и переходов вдвое
  больше, чем с компьютера. Мобильная версия — правильное решение.
* Самый ценный адрес — **главная страница**: 15 переходов из 47.
* Самый частый запрос — **«rempire»**, 141 показ, 11 переходов, среднее место
  3,79. Свой собственный магазин по своему имени должен быть первым.

**Что сделано к переезду.** Все старые адреса Shopify — 473 штуки, которые
Google знает прямо сейчас, — теперь ведут на новые страницы. 403 из них
открывают ровно тот товар, который человек искал; ни один не открывает
«Страница не найдена». Язык сохраняется: кто пришёл на эстонский адрес,
попадёт на эстонскую страницу.

**Чего ждать после переезда.**

1. **Первые две-три недели показов станет меньше.** Это нормально: Google
   заново обходит сайт и переносит вес со старых адресов на новые. Пугаться не
   нужно, но и «через неделю проверим» — рано.
2. **Через месяц-полтора позиции должны вернуться** и, если ничего не сломано,
   стать чуть лучше — новые страницы быстрее, у каждой свой заголовок, описание
   и цена.
3. **Карточки товаров в Google Покупках пропадут сразу.** Их присылало
   приложение Shopify, вместе с Shopify оно и уйдёт. Потери почти нет: за
   неделю они принесли 3 перехода из 47. Если захотим их вернуть — это
   отдельная настройка Google Merchant Center, и лучше сразу ограничить показ
   Эстонией, Латвией, Литвой и Финляндией.
4. **Товары, которых больше нет в продаже** (219 старых адресов), будут вести
   на страницу бренда или на «Все товары». Google со временем уберёт их из
   поиска — так и должно быть.
5. **Что стоит сделать руками, а не кодом:** эстонские запросы вроде
   «habemehooldus», «juuksevaha», «habemeolid» — магазин по ним на 40–60-м
   месте. Их закрывают статьи в блоге на эстонском, а не настройки.

## 7. Questions for Dim

1. **The site root.** `/` is a **307** (temporary) to `/shop2/`, on purpose:
   `next.config.ts` says the shop is expected to *become* the root later. But
   `/` is the best-earning URL on the property — 15 of the 47 clicks, position
   3.37, and the query behind it is `rempire`. A temporary redirect asks Google
   to keep indexing `/` and to pass nothing on. **Is the shop going to move to
   `/` before the switch, or not?** If not, make it a 308 and let `/shop2/`
   inherit the brand ranking. If yes, leave the 307 exactly as it is.
2. **The `/shop/p/<id>/` pages.** `docs/seo.md` already flags this as "a
   decision, not a leftover": 95 legacy product pages are still served at
   `/shop/p/…` with their own link previews. They are not in the sitemap and
   Google has never indexed them (no `/shop/` URL appears in the export), but
   they are a second address for every product. **301 them to `/shop2/p/<id>/`
   at the switch?**
3. **The merchant listings.** 736 impressions and 3 clicks came from Shopify's
   free product feed, 97 % of them shown outside the delivery area. The feed
   dies with Shopify. `tools/build-merchant-feed.mjs` already builds a Google
   Merchant Center feed for the new shop. **Do we submit it — and if so, do we
   restrict the target countries to EE/LV/LT/FI?** My recommendation: yes and
   yes. Showing a Tallinn shop to Dubai is what produced a week of position-1
   results worth nothing.
4. **Finland.** 25 impressions at position 8.6 and not one click, all week, in a
   country the shop delivers to. Everything on those pages is in Russian,
   Estonian or English. **Is Finnish worth a fourth language, or is Finland a
   delivery destination rather than a market?**
5. **The refund policy contradicts itself.** It promises a 30-day return with a
   return label, then excludes "personal care goods (such as beauty products)" —
   which is nearly the whole catalogue. It is also Shopify boilerplate in
   English, and it is now a page Google indexes. Two things follow: it needs
   fixing as *text*, and until it is fixed we cannot publish
   `hasMerchantReturnPolicy`, which is what puts "Free 30-day returns" under a
   result. **What is the real policy?** (EU law gives 14 days on distance
   selling; the exclusion for opened cosmetics is real but narrower than this
   text makes it.)
6. **Shipping in the structured data.** «Free delivery» under a search result is
   one of the strongest things a shop can show. The facts exist — an Estonian
   parcel is 5.47 €, free from 59 € — but they differ by carrier and country.
   **May I publish `shippingDetails` for Estonia only (5.47 €, free from 59 €),
   or do you want the full four-country table first?**
7. **Two identities.** `docs/seo.md` has flagged this since 03.09 and it is now
   *published*: the policy texts trade as `REMPIRE (THEFLOW OÜ)`, registrikood
   16320586; the footer, the invoices and — as of this branch — the
   `Organization` structured data say `Rempire Store OÜ`, 12216136. Google is
   now being told the second one. **Which company is the shop?**
8. **`/collections/system-4` → the System 4 brand page.** That collection is the
   third best-earning URL (106 impressions, 2 clicks). I send it to
   `/shop2/b/system-4/`. The alternative is `/shop2/c/hair/`. **Brand page is my
   recommendation** — it lists the same products the collection did — but it is
   your call, and there are 104 more collection handles mapped the same way in
   `src/lib/legacy-redirects.ts` if you want to read them.
9. **Two hops on every old link.** `trailingSlash: true` makes every legacy URL
   a 308 → 301 chain. Harmless to Google, one extra round trip for a shopper.
   Removing it means taking over trailing-slash normalisation for the whole
   site. **Leave it?** My recommendation: yes.
10. **A visible change I did not make.** The out-of-stock product page keeps its
    rich result and its place in the sitemap, and now says «нет в наличии» in
    the search snippet. Renat may prefer that an out-of-stock product simply not
    be findable. That would be a UI/indexing decision, not a code one —
    **should an out-of-stock product stay in Google?** (My recommendation: yes;
    it is 5 products of 220 and «Сообщить о наличии» turns the visit into a
    subscriber.)

## 8. What to verify at the switch

Everything in `docs/seo.md` § "At the switch" still applies. Add to it:

```bash
# after the DNS moves, one of each shape, and no 404 anywhere
for u in /products/system-4-bio-botanical-shampoo \
         /ru/products/system-4-bio-botanical-serum \
         /et/products/hair-resort-spray \
         /en-lv/products/xerjoff-1861-naxos-eau-de-parfum-100ml \
         /collections/system-4 /et/collections/habemeolid \
         /policies/shipping-policy /et/pages/contact /blogs/blog /et; do
  curl -sIL -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code} %{url_effective}  <- $u\n" "https://rempireshop.com$u"
done
```

In Search Console, in this order: submit `sitemap.xml`; use **URL Inspection →
Live test** on `/shop2/` and on two products before requesting indexing; watch
**Coverage** for the old `/products/…` URLs moving to *Page with redirect*
(expect two to three weeks); watch **International Targeting** for "no return
tags"; and expect the *Данные о товарах продавца* row to go to zero the day the
Shopify feed stops — that is 736 impressions and 3 clicks disappearing, and it
is not a regression.
